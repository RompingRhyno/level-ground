# Service copy brief

Source material, rules, and a line-by-line audit of the current drafts — for whoever writes the next
pass over the twelve service descriptions on `/landscape-design-installation` and
`/landscape-maintenance`.

Everything in sections 3–7 is evidence: the old site's own words, quoted verbatim, plus the covers each
project now carries on the new site. Section 8 lists claims the current drafts make that the evidence
does **not** support.

---

## 1. The rules

1. **Write fresh copy.** The old site's sentences are reference material, not a template. Same facts,
   new writing. Do not lightly reword the old prose — it reads like 2016 SEO and it was written for a
   different page structure.
2. **Purposeful, not pushy.** No selling, no superlatives, no urgency. The reader is a homeowner
   deciding whether to call, and the copy's job is to help them recognise their own project in it.
3. **Respect the reader's time — ease of reading and length.** Two sentences, roughly 25–45 words.
   Plain language, concrete nouns, one idea per sentence.
4. **Avoid unfounded claims unless there is a strong basis that it applies.** This is the strictest
   rule. In practice: if the old site, the project descriptions, or the operator has not said it,
   either leave it out or state it as something we offer rather than something we did. Section 8 is the
   current list of violations.
5. **The goal is the website experience**, not fidelity to the original text. If a fact is thin or
   confusing, prefer plain and true over detailed and shaky.

## 2. AI tells to avoid

Known patterns, several of which are in the current drafts:

- **"Not X, but Y" / "not just X — Y" constructions.** *"A healthy lawn is a routine, not a rescue."*
  *"The garden gets the schedule, not the other way around."* Both are in the drafts and both should go.
- **The balanced aphorism**, especially as a closing line. *"a plant is only as good as the ground it
  starts in"*. One per page maximum, and only if it is actually true and specific.
- **Em-dash overuse.** The current drafts lean on them heavily; in quantity they read as machine-written.
- **Tricolons / rule of three** in every sentence. *"lawn, beds, containers"*, *"pruning and hedging,
  seasonal planting, and the fall leaf rounds"*.
- **"designed to", "so you can", "whether you're X or Y", "elevate", "seamless", "curated",
  "tailored", "nestled", "boasts".** None appear in the drafts; keep it that way.
- **Symmetry for its own sake** — every sentence the same shape and length.
- **Abstract benefit stacking** with no object in sight (*"we create spaces that inspire"*).
- **Empty intensifiers**: *truly, genuinely, perfectly, beautifully*.

Voice reference: the operator's own writing on the old site is plain and specific — *"Replaced a 730
sq. ft. backyard lawn with a grid of 24" x 24" grey concrete pavers with ground cover."* Aim closer to
that than to marketing copy.

## 6. Claims the current drafts make that the evidence does not support

Read this before writing. Each line is a claim currently live on the site, and what the source actually
supports.

| Claim in the current draft | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Pavers: *"Grades and drainage are set first, so a rain-soaked winter does not shift what we build."* | **Unsupported.** No page or project description mentions base preparation, grading or drainage. | The one adjacent fact is a *failure* of the old work: West 31st's patio pavers had sunk. |
| Rock & gravel: *"for drainage and dry creek beds"* | **Unsupported.** No project mentions drainage or creek beds. | What the projects do show: gravel set *with paving slabs* (Cypress), decorative river rock (East Broadway), boulders placed as accents (West 11th). |
| Decking: *"built on a frame sized and finished for how you will actually use it"* | **Unsupported.** Generic trade talk, nothing behind it. | Alder describes a *"wood patio deck"*; nothing about frames or sizing. |
| Fencing: *"Cedar fences built to last"* | **Misattributed.** Durability is the *aluminum* claim, and the old copy attaches it there specifically. | Old copy: *"customized lifetime aluminum fence that weathers all seasons"*; cedar is described only as *"pre-fab cedar fence structure"*. |
| Outdoor Lighting: *"Low-voltage lighting"* | **Unsupported.** No mention of low-voltage anywhere. | Documented: garden spotlights (Dunbar St, Cartier), lighting for "day or night" use (Duke St), accent + driveway/walkway security (old copy). |
| Irrigation: *"set zone by zone"* | **Unsupported.** No mention of zoning or controllers. | Documented: *"automatic irrigation or a drip line system"* (old copy) — one project, West 31st. |
| Retaining walls: *"We build engineered block and stack walls"* (service-wide) | **Overclaims.** Only West Vancouver's wall is documented as engineered. | West Vancouver: *"Engineered walls had to be built to retain two tiers of lawn area"*. The other four projects say plain *"Retaining wall"*. |
| Garden maintenance: *"on a schedule that fits the garden"* | Fine as an offering, not as history. | Old copy documents a *"scheduled garden maintenance program"* with weekly/bi-weekly cadence for lawn care. |

**Supported and safe to keep or reuse:**
micro clover vs chafer beetle and weed suppression, and the time/maintenance saving (old copy, verbatim);
artificial turf for shade, drainage trouble and hard-to-mow areas, and its pet/child friendliness (old
copy, verbatim); sod as straightforward new lawn; putting green (Cartier); raised beds and vegetable
boxes (Alder, West 9th, West 35th, East Broadway); retaining walls in *concrete block and stack systems*
(old copy) with a raised bed or wall garden on top (old copy); aluminum fencing as the lifetime,
low-maintenance option (old copy); lighting for accent, driveway/walkway security, and evening use;
automatic irrigation and drip lines (old copy, one project); the full garden-maintenance task list
(cultivation and weeding, pruning, planting, hedging, leaf racking and blowing of walkways and
driveways, lawn and garden bed renovation) and the lawn-care list (mowing, seasonal aerating,
fertilization, power raking moss control, over-seeding); topsoil and mulch as things they actually
supply.

**Do not claim, anywhere, without new evidence:** engineered walls for projects other than West
Vancouver; low-voltage lighting; irrigation zoning; drainage or grade preparation; cedar durability;
dry creek beds; deck framing; years in business; certifications, awards or insurance; crew size;
"no-maintenance" anything.

## 7. Details worth using (all sourced)

- **Specifics beat adjectives.** 730 sq ft of lawn replaced with 24" × 24" grey concrete pavers
  (Wiltshire); 5-ft English Yew hedge installed for privacy (Duke); a putting green (Cartier); two
  tiers of lawn retained behind engineered walls (West Vancouver); an old underground oil tank removed
  before the yard was usable (West 24th); red bricks salvaged and reused (West 24th); a walkway
  renewed because it was a trip hazard (Bayswater).
- **Real client situations**, which the old descriptions use freely: crows and raccoons destroying a
  lawn looking for beetle larvae (Duke); a couple expecting a second baby wanting low maintenance (East
  12th); a family and dog with barely enough space (Dunbar); a dog-friendly boulevard (Cypress); a
  young couple with a small child and a dog wanting a fire pit (Dunbar St).
- **Local geography the copy can rely on**: Kitsilano (West 11th), West Vancouver with views and
  elevation (West Vancouver), a townhouse complex (Alder), a condo with drainage issues (East
  Broadway), boulevards (Cypress, West 11th).
- **The site already leans on this material elsewhere** — the home page hero says *"Custom outdoor
  spaces built to last."* Reusing that promise in a service description makes it a claim, so don't.
